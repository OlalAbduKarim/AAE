# ANALYTICA AUTONOMOUS ENGINE (AAE)
## Master Architecture Specification
**Organisation:** Analytica Technologies  
**Project:** Analytica Autonomous Engine  
**Version:** 0.1 — Initial Engineering Specification

---

## 1. Mission

Build a proprietary hybrid neural-symbolic autonomous intelligence specialising in statistics, data science and scientific research.

AAE must be capable of understanding analytical problems, examining datasets, evaluating possible statistical approaches, selecting scientifically defensible methods, executing analyses, independently verifying results and communicating findings.

The objective is to develop a specialist analytical engine with the depth, reliability and decision-making discipline that Stockfish demonstrates in chess.

AAE is not intended to be a general-purpose chatbot or a collection of automated statistical scripts.

## 2. Core design principles

1. Scientific correctness takes priority over producing an answer.
2. Neural intelligence must learn from data, experience and expert-labelled scenarios.
3. Symbolic reasoning must enforce mathematical, statistical and scientific constraints.
4. Every analytical decision must have an explainable justification.
5. Numerical results must be independently verified.
6. The engine must recognise uncertainty and know when information is insufficient.
7. Original datasets must remain preserved.
8. Every transformation and analytical decision must be traceable.
9. Models must be benchmarked against conventional methods.
10. Learning and model updates must be controlled and measurable.

## 3. Core architecture

AAE consists of seven interconnected subsystems.

### A. Neural Intelligence

Proprietary trained models responsible for:

- Understanding natural-language research questions.
- Recognising variables, measurement scales and dataset structures.
- Learning statistical method suitability.
- Detecting unusual patterns and potential anomalies.
- Interpreting verified analytical findings.
- Learning from reviewed analytical scenarios.

### B. Symbolic Scientific Reasoning

A formal knowledge and constraint system responsible for:

- Statistical assumptions.
- Mathematical relationships.
- Method eligibility.
- Study-design requirements.
- Sample structure.
- Measurement scales.
- Missing-data considerations.
- Statistical decision rules.
- Scientific limitations.

It must be capable of rejecting recommendations that violate established requirements.

### C. Data Intelligence

Responsible for:

- Data ingestion.
- Dataset profiling.
- Variable recognition.
- Data-quality assessment.
- Missing-data analysis.
- Duplicate detection.
- Distribution analysis.
- Relationships between variables.
- Auditable cleaning proposals.

### D. Statistical Computation Engine

Responsible for executing verified scientific methods, including:

- Descriptive statistics.
- Probability and distributions.
- Statistical inference.
- Hypothesis testing.
- Regression.
- Multivariate analysis.
- Bayesian statistics.
- Time-series analysis.
- Survey statistics.
- Machine learning.
- Experimental and observational study analysis.

### E. Autonomous Control Engine

Responsible for:

- Understanding the analytical objective.
- Breaking complex problems into tasks.
- Coordinating neural and symbolic reasoning.
- Selecting and evaluating analytical plans.
- Requesting clarification.
- Managing execution.
- Handling failures.
- Determining when to stop.

### F. Verification Engine

Responsible for:

- Independent numerical verification.
- Assumption diagnostics.
- Mathematical consistency checks.
- Reference dataset comparisons.
- Detection of unsupported conclusions.
- Reproducibility testing.

### G. Learning and Evaluation System

Responsible for:

- Training datasets.
- Model training.
- Benchmarking.
- Experiment tracking.
- Model versioning.
- Expert feedback.
- Controlled model improvements.

## 4. Analytical decision cycle

Every research task must follow this general process:

1. Receive the research question and dataset.
2. Understand the analytical objective.
3. Inspect and profile the dataset.
4. Identify relevant variables and study structure.
5. Identify missing information.
6. Generate candidate analytical approaches.
7. Evaluate candidates using learned intelligence and scientific constraints.
8. Select a defensible analytical plan.
9. Request clarification or approval when necessary.
10. Execute the selected analysis.
11. Independently verify results.
12. Interpret verified findings.
13. Generate reproducible outputs and reports.

The engine must have three possible decision outcomes:

- PROCEED: sufficient information and defensible analytical plan.
- REVIEW REQUIRED: clarification, warnings or approval needed.
- REJECT: the requested analysis is invalid or cannot answer the stated question as requested.

## 5. Neural model family

Initial proposed specialist models:

- AAE-NLU: analytical language understanding.
- AAE-DATA: dataset and variable understanding.
- AAE-METHOD: statistical method evaluation.
- AAE-ANOMALY: anomaly and data-quality intelligence.
- AAE-INTERPRET: interpretation of verified results.

AAE-CONTROL coordinates these components and may incorporate learned decision policies after a reliable control system and benchmark environment exist.

Architecture selection must be evidence-driven. Neural models must outperform appropriate conventional baselines before being adopted.

## 6. Statistical integrity

AAE must never allow a language model or neural network to invent or directly fabricate statistical calculations.

Numerical computations must use verified scientific algorithms.

The engine must distinguish:

- Statistical significance from practical significance.
- Association from causation.
- Prediction from explanation.
- Missing information from negative evidence.
- Unusual observations from confirmed errors.
- Technical executability from scientific validity.

## 7. Deployment architecture

AAE must ultimately support:

1. Standalone local execution.
2. Cloud-hosted execution.
3. API integration.
4. Organisational and enterprise licensing.
5. Controlled deployment of model versions.

The local and cloud products must use the same underlying scientific engine.

## 8. Development philosophy

Development must proceed through independently testable milestones.

No subsystem is considered complete merely because it runs.

Each must demonstrate:

- Correctness.
- Reliability.
- Measurable performance.
- Reproducibility.
- Documented limitations.
- Successful benchmark evaluation.

The final product must be judged by its analytical performance on unfamiliar research problems, not its size, number of scripts or appearance.
